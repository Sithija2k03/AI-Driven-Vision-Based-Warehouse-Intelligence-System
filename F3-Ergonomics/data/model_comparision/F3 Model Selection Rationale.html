Why the Hybrid Architecture?
🎯

YOLOv11 detects, MediaPipe refines

YOLOv11-Pose handles multi-person detection in a wide-angle CCTV scene. MediaPipe then runs on each cropped person to add 16 extra landmarks (heel, foot toe, face mesh points) that YOLO's COCO skeleton omits entirely.

🦶

Heel & foot landmarks for ergonomics

Accurate ankle dorsiflexion, knee valgus, and foot progression angle require heel and toe keypoints. These exist only in MediaPipe's 33-point BlazePose schema — not in YOLO's 17-point COCO output. Omitting them would make lower-body ergonomic scoring impossible.

🏭

CCTV occlusion is unavoidable

In real warehouse footage, workers are partially hidden by racking, forklifts, and each other. When YOLO keypoints fall below confidence threshold, the 3-zone freeze-state system holds the last valid posture per body region rather than corrupting the score — something MediaPipe alone cannot provide across a scene.

⚡

v9 & v10 eliminated immediately

YOLOv9s and YOLOv10s showed no keypoint output in benchmarks — they are detection-only models without a pose head. They cannot be used for skeleton extraction, and are therefore disqualified from F3 regardless of their detection rate or speed figures.

📐

Why not MediaPipe alone?

MediaPipe is a single-person model: it cannot detect or separate multiple workers in a scene frame. It also fails at the viewing distances typical of warehouse CCTV (3–10 m). It contributes landmark richness; it cannot substitute for YOLO's scene-level detection.

🔬

YOLOv11 beats v8 on real data

Against the same 122 CCTV frames, YOLOv11m-pose achieved detection confidence 0.644 vs 0.580 for v8m — a +10.9% quality improvement. YOLOv11 also detected at a higher rate (63.1% vs 56.6%) while producing fewer but more reliable bounding boxes, reducing false positives in crowded warehouse scenes.

✅

Final Architecture Verdict

YOLOv11m-Pose is the strongest pose-capable detector across all four candidates benchmarked on 122 real warehouse CCTV frames. YOLOv9s and YOLOv10s were immediately disqualified — no keypoint output. Against YOLOv8m-pose, YOLOv11m delivers higher detection confidence (0.644 vs 0.580), better detection rate (63.1% vs 56.6%), and comparable keypoint confidence (0.634 vs 0.633) with no disadvantage. MediaPipe is not a replacement but a complement: its 33-point BlazePose schema provides the heel, toe, and foot landmarks that YOLO's COCO-17 schema cannot supply, enabling accurate lower-body ergonomic angles. The hybrid is the minimum viable architecture to meet F3's ergonomic scoring requirements in a real warehouse CCTV context — neither model alone is sufficient.
